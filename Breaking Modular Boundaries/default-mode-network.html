What Is the DMN?

The Default Mode Network (DMN) is a collection of interconnected brain regions involved in internally directed thought, autobiographical memory, and self-referential processing. The essay connects persistent self-focused thinking and rumination with depression and anxiety, and describes research linking depression and obsessive-compulsive disorder with unusually rigid patterns of brain activity. These associations do not mean the DMN alone causes these conditions (Timmermann et al., 2023).

Psychedelics and Brain Connectivity

DMT interacts with the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor, and psychedelic states can change how brain regions communicate. The essay discusses DMT neuroimaging research that found altered brain activity and connectivity, including in networks associated with the DMN (Timmermann et al., 2023). These findings describe changes during the psychedelic experience; they do not by themselves show that DMN changes cause therapeutic improvement.

Ego Dissolution

Ego dissolution describes a reported weakening of the usual sense of a separate, continuous self. The essay presents this experience as one possible way people may reconsider recurring thoughts and emotional patterns. The REBUS model proposes that psychedelics may temporarily loosen rigid beliefs, but this remains a theoretical explanation—not proof that ego dissolution or DMN changes are the cause of healing (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019).
