What is DMT?

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a naturally occurring psychedelic compound that has been studied for its interactions with serotonin receptors and its effects on human consciousness. It occurs in some plants used to prepare ayahuasca and has also been reported in human tissue, although its normal physiological role is not yet established (Barker, 2018).

Serotonin Receptors

Research has identified the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor as an important component of the neurological effects associated with classic psychedelics. DMT's interaction with this receptor is thought to contribute to changes in brain activity and communication between regions. EEG-fMRI research has examined these effects during the DMT experience (Timmermann et al., 2023), but observed brain changes do not by themselves demonstrate a therapeutic benefit.

Therapeutic Research

Researchers are investigating the potential therapeutic applications of DMT in professionally supervised psychedelic-assisted therapy. The essay describes this approach as promising but still under-researched. Important limitations include intense acute effects and possible increases in heart rate, blood pressure, or psychological distress; risks may be greater for people vulnerable to psychosis (Barker, 2018). Blinding is also difficult because participants can often recognize the psychedelic effects, making it harder to separate drug effects from expectations. Clinical findings from psilocybin trials provide context for psychedelic therapy, but do not establish DMT's effectiveness (Goodwin et al., 2022).
