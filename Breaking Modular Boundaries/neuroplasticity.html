Understanding Neuroplasticity

Neuroplasticity refers to the brain's ability to modify its connections and organization in response to experience and learning. The research essay considers whether this capacity could help explain how psychedelic-assisted therapy might support changes in persistent patterns of thought.

Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor

Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) is a protein involved in neuronal survival, development, and synaptic plasticity. The essay describes BDNF as one possible pathway through which psychedelics could support the growth and remodeling of neural connections. However, increased plasticity is a proposed biological mechanism, not evidence on its own that a person will experience lasting symptom relief.

Psychedelics and Plasticity Research

Experimental research suggests that some psychedelic compounds can promote structural and functional neural plasticity (Ly et al., 2018). The essay connects these findings to the possibility that DMT could help the brain form new associations after an acute psychedelic experience. Much of the evidence for these plasticity mechanisms is preclinical, however; whether they lead to durable therapeutic benefits in people remains an open question.
