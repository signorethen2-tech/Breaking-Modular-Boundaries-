What Is Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy?

Psychedelic-assisted therapy is an emerging research area investigating psychedelic compounds alongside structured psychological support. In the approach described in the essay, a small number of medically supervised dosing sessions are combined with preparation and follow-up therapy, rather than relying on continuous medication alone.

Potential Applications

Researchers are investigating whether PAT could help people with conditions such as depression, PTSD, and anxiety, including cases that have not responded to other treatments. The essay notes that DMT's acute effects can be brief, roughly 10-15 minutes in the administration context it discusses—which may be a practical consideration for supervised sessions. A short experience does not, by itself, demonstrate lasting benefit.

Risks and Limitations

Important considerations include acute psychological effects, individual differences, psychiatric screening, medical supervision, and uncertainty about long-term outcomes. The essay identifies possible increases in heart rate and blood pressure, acute psychological distress, and concern about triggering psychosis in people with certain vulnerabilities. It also discusses the possibility of persistent perceptual effects (Barker, 2018).

Evidence and Uncertainty

Research involving psilocybin and other psychedelics should not automatically be interpreted as direct clinical evidence for DMT-based therapy. The clinical trials discussed in the essay examine psilocybin, while DMT-specific research cited here includes neuroimaging that examines brain effects rather than proving treatment outcomes (Goodwin et al., 2022; Griffiths et al., 2016; Timmermann et al., 2023). Difficulties with placebo and blinding also mean that expectations may influence reported outcomes.
